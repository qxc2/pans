My research has focused on understanding and improving the separation of critical elements such as rare earths, Co/Ni, V and Cr. During my Ph.D. in Chemical Engineering, I developed solvent-extraction and multiphase separation strategies for rare earth and transition-metal systems, including Pr/Nd separation and rare earth recovery from spent NdFeB magnets. In my subsequent research at the University of Chicago, the University of Illinois Chicago, and Oak Ridge National Laboratory, I studied the molecular and interfacial mechanisms that govern metal-ion adsorption, complexation, aggregation, and transport. Using synchrotron X-ray scattering, surface-sensitive spectroscopy, molecular dynamics simulations, and complementary analytical methods, I investigated how aqueous speciation, interfacial organization, metastable precipitation, and transport barriers influence separation efficiency and selectivity. These works have resulted in publications in journals including Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, Journal of the American Chemical Society, ACS Central Science, and Environmental Science & Technology.

At Ames National Laboratory, I have continued to move this research toward more sustainable critical-material recovery. My recent work has examined ion-specific precipitation for rare earth separation and wastewater remediation, as well as adsorption and selective desorption of rare earths using mineral sorbents and alternative solvents. These projects have strengthened my interest in developing separation processes that not only recover valuable materials, but also reduce solvent use, chemical consumption, and secondary waste generation.
